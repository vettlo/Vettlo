What Is Accounting Software? What It Does and Who Needs It

By Vettlo Editorial TeamUpdated Nov 22, 2026 · Prices verified Nov 22, 2026

Short answer

Accounting software is a program, now usually a cloud app, that records every money movement in a business as double-entry ledger entries and turns them into financial statements. It imports bank and card transactions, sends invoices, tracks bills, reconciles accounts and produces the profit and loss and balance sheet your accountant and tax return rely on. Any business that has outgrown a spreadsheet of income and expenses, or shares its books with an accountant, is the typical user.

What is accounting software?

Accounting software is the system a business uses to keep its books: it records income, spending, what customers owe and what the business owes, then summarizes all of it in reports. It replaces the paper ledger and the homemade spreadsheet, and it writes the debits and credits for you each time you save an invoice, a bill or a bank transaction.

If you're ready to pick a product, our ranking of the best accounting software compares 6 tools on price and features. This guide explains what the category is, what it does day to day, and how it differs from the tools it's often confused with.

Most small businesses now use cloud accounting software that runs in a browser and a phone app, billed monthly. Older desktop programs installed on one computer still exist, and Intuit stopped selling most QuickBooks Desktop editions to new US customers after September 30, 2024; our guide to whether QuickBooks Desktop is being discontinued covers that change.

The ledger is the part that makes it accounting software rather than an invoicing app: every other job writes into it, which is why the reports always tie back to the same numbers. The mechanics are covered in how double-entry bookkeeping works, and the choice of reporting basis in cash vs accrual accounting.

Around that core, plans add extras such as inventory, project costing, multi-currency, budgets and approvals; the plan table further down shows where they start. Payroll is usually a separate product or add-on that posts its totals into the ledger, rather than part of the accounting plan itself.

Accounting software vs bookkeeping software, spreadsheets and ERP

Accounting software sits between a spreadsheet, which only lists transactions, and an ERP system, which runs a whole company's operations. "Bookkeeping software" is mostly a marketing label for the same products, aimed at the person entering transactions rather than the accountant reviewing them.

What are the main types of accounting software?

Small business accounting software comes in four broad types, and most buyers can rule out two of them quickly based on size and how they bill customers.

  • General small business accounting. Full ledger, bank feeds, invoices, bills and reports, with higher plans for inventory and projects. QuickBooks, Xero and Zoho Books are the main examples.
  • Invoicing-first accounting for service businesses. Built around clients, time and proposals, with the ledger in the background. FreshBooks is the example on our list; its plans are capped by the number of billable clients.
  • Free accounting software. Zoho Books and Wave have free plans in our ranking; they cover the core books but put things like automatic bank imports or higher revenue on paid tiers.
  • Mid-market financial management. Multi-entity accounting and deeper reporting for a finance team. Sage Intacct is in this group, priced by quote.

Who needs accounting software?

The IRS doesn't require accounting software: it says you "may choose any recordkeeping system suited to your business that clearly shows your income and expenses," and that electronic systems must meet the same basic principles as paper books. In practice, most businesses switch from a spreadsheet once the record-keeping load or the number of people involved grows.

Signs a spreadsheet has stopped working:

  1. You send more than a handful of invoices a month and lose track of which are paid.
  2. You have bills to schedule, not just card purchases.
  3. More than one person touches the books, including an outside bookkeeper or accountant.
  4. You need a balance sheet for a loan, an investor or a business tax return.
  5. Bank statements no longer match your totals and you can't find out why.
  6. You collect sales tax or carry inventory.

Whatever system you use, the IRS's supporting-document rules don't change: keep invoices, deposit records, receipts, canceled checks or other proof of payment for income, purchases, expenses and assets. Its general rule is to keep records for 3 years, longer in some cases, and employment tax records for at least 4 years after the tax is due or paid, whichever is later. Most accounting software lets you attach receipts and bills to the transaction they support, which is the main recordkeeping advantage over a spreadsheet.

Summarizes IRS guidance checked Nov 22, 2026; this isn't tax or legal advice.

FAQ

Is Excel accounting software?

No. Excel is a spreadsheet, so it records whatever you type but doesn't post balanced entries, import bank transactions or produce a balance sheet on its own. Many very small businesses keep their books in a spreadsheet, and the IRS accepts any system that clearly shows income and expenses.

Does a small business have to use accounting software?

No. The IRS lets you choose any recordkeeping system that clearly shows your income and expenses, on paper or electronic. Electronic records must meet the same requirements as paper ones, and you still need the supporting documents such as invoices, receipts and proof of payment.

What is the difference between accounting software and bookkeeping software?

In practice they're the same products. Bookkeeping is the day-to-day recording of transactions, and accounting is analyzing and reporting on them; the software does both, and vendors use whichever label suits their audience.

Can my accountant log in to my accounting software?

Usually yes, at no extra cost. QuickBooks Online plans include 2 or more accountant users, and Xero doesn't charge per user, so an accountant can be added without raising the bill.
