How we vet

Our review methodology

Choosing business software is expensive to get wrong. Here is exactly how we build a shortlist, so you can judge it yourself.

6 stepsWeighted criteria per categoryPrices checked at the sourceLast updated Oct 2, 2026
  1. 1

    Build the long list

    For each category we start from every product a team could reasonably buy: vendor directories, review platforms (G2, Capterra, TrustRadius), practitioner communities and reader suggestions. A tool does not need any relationship with us to be considered.

  2. 2

    Set criteria for the category

    We write criteria specific to the job the software does. A dialer is judged on dialing modes and CRM sync; a CRM on pipeline management and reporting; a payroll tool on tax filing and compliance. Each criterion gets a published weight, shown on the review.

  3. 3

    Test hands-on

    We sign up for trials or free plans and run the workflows a real team would: setting up a project, importing contacts, building an automation, running a report. We also read each vendor's current pricing page, plan limits and documentation, and note exactly which plan a feature sits on and what's an add-on.

  4. 4

    Check against real users

    We compare our findings with patterns in verified user reviews and with practitioners in our network, looking for consistent strengths and complaints across company sizes.

  5. 5

    Score and rank

    Each tool gets a 0 to 5 rating per criterion. Weighted totals become a score out of 100. Tools below 70 are usually not included in the ranked list; we list them under Also considered with their score. Position follows score, adjusted only by editorial judgment we explain in the review. The exception: partner compensation may affect which products we include and where they appear, as disclosed on every review.

  6. 6

    Re-check every 90 days

    We re-verify pricing, plans and major features at least every 90 days and show the date on each review. Corrections are logged at the bottom of the page.

Editorial standards

Independence

Editorial decisions are made by the editorial team. People who sell advertising do not assign, edit or approve reviews.

Accuracy

Prices, plan limits and feature claims are checked against the vendor's current public pages or our own test account. Every review shows its last verified date.

Corrections

When we get something wrong, we fix it and note the change in the review's change log. Report an error through our contact page.
