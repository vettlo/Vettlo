5 Best Jenkins Alternatives in 2026

Short answer

The best Jenkins alternative for most teams is GitHub Actions (90/100). 2,000 free minutes a month; standard runners free in public repos; self-hosted runners are free.

  • GitHub Actions (90/100): best if your code is on GitHub.
  • CircleCI (87/100): best hosted service for fast, parallel builds.
  • GitLab (84/100): best all-in-one platform.
  • Buildkite (82/100): best for keeping builds on your own machines.
  • Azure Pipelines (81/100): best for Microsoft shops.

Jenkins is free and runs anywhere, but someone has to install, update and secure the server and its plugins. Teams usually look elsewhere when that upkeep costs more engineering time than a hosted service would cost in fees.

Why teams look for Jenkins alternatives

  • Server upkeep. You patch Jenkins, its plugins and the build machines yourself.
  • Plugin risk. Most features come from more than 2,300 community plugins, which vary in quality and need their own updates.
  • Learning curve. Jenkins rates lowest on ease of use in our ranking (2.5/5).
  • Community support only. Help comes from the Jenkins community; the hosted tools here include ticket or email support on paid plans.

The alternatives

1

GitHub Actions

Best if your code is on GitHub
Visit website ↗
Starting price$4/user/moGitHub Team, 3,000 minutes a month; Enterprise $21 with 50,000 minutes
Free trial / planFree plan (2,000 minutes a month; public repos free)
Vettlo score90/100
G2 user rating4.7/52,418 reviews

GitHub Actions is the CI/CD built into GitHub, with workflows defined in YAML next to the code and thousands of reusable actions. The Free plan includes 2,000 minutes a month, Team ($4 per user) 3,000 and Enterprise ($21) 50,000. Extra minutes cost $0.006 on a 2-core Linux runner, $0.010 on Windows and $0.062 on macOS, and self-hosted runners don't use the allowance.

What you gain over Jenkins

  • 2,000 free minutes a month; standard runners free in public repos
  • Self-hosted runners are free

What you give up

  • macOS minutes cost $0.062
  • SAML SSO needs Enterprise ($21)

Skip it if: Your code lives in GitLab or Bitbucket and you want CI in the same place.

CircleCI

Best hosted service for fast, parallel builds

Starting price$15/moPerformance, 5 active users; extra users $15 each; credits $15 per 25,000

CircleCI is a cloud CI/CD service that works with GitHub, GitLab and Bitbucket repos and bills compute in credits by the second. Free includes 30,000 credits a month, 5 active users and up to 30 concurrent jobs; Performance starts at $15 a month, sells credits at $15 per 25,000 and raises concurrency to 80. Scale, priced by quote, adds SAML SSO, config policies and unlimited concurrency.

What you gain over Jenkins

  • 30,000 free credits and 30 concurrent jobs on Free
  • Works with GitHub, GitLab and Bitbucket

What you give up

  • Credit billing is harder to predict
  • SSO needs Scale, by quote

Skip it if: You want a flat per-user price with minutes bundled in.

Jenkins alternatives FAQ

What is the best alternative to Jenkins?

GitHub Actions if your code is on GitHub, with 2,000 free minutes a month and free self-hosted runners. CircleCI is the strongest standalone hosted service, and Buildkite suits teams that want to keep running builds on their own machines without maintaining a Jenkins server.

Is there a free alternative to Jenkins?

Yes, for smaller workloads. GitHub's Free plan includes 2,000 minutes a month, CircleCI's 30,000 credits for 5 users, Buildkite's 2,000 hosted minutes for 5 users, Azure Pipelines 1,800 minutes plus 1 free self-hosted job, and GitLab 400 minutes for 5 users.

Can I keep my own build servers if I leave Jenkins?

Yes. GitHub Actions self-hosted runners are free, Buildkite Pro includes 10 self-hosted agents then $3.50 each a month, Azure Pipelines charges $15 per extra self-hosted job, and GitLab and CircleCI also support self-hosted runners.
