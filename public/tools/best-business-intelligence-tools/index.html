Short answer

  • Microsoft Power BI (92/100): best overall and best value for most companies.

Business intelligence (BI) tools connect to your databases, spreadsheets and business apps, model the data and turn it into dashboards and reports people can filter and share. Every tool here builds charts and dashboards, so the differences are in how they charge (per user, per role, per data capacity or by usage credits), what the free version can share, how much data a plan holds and which plan adds row-level security, embedding and SSO.

We scored seven BI tools on six weighted criteria. For each one we list the free option, the entry paid price and billing basis, and the plan where governance and embedding start.

Best overall and best value for most companies

Microsoft Power BI is a BI platform with a free Windows app, Power BI Desktop, for modeling data and building reports, and a cloud service for publishing and sharing them. The free account is for personal analysis; Pro ($14 per user a month, paid yearly) adds publishing and sharing, with a 1 GB model memory limit and 8 refreshes a day; Premium Per User ($24) raises that to 100 GB and 48 refreshes a day and adds advanced AI, dataflows and XMLA read/write. Fabric capacity plans let viewers use reports without a per-user license from F64 up.

Who it's best for

Companies on Microsoft 365 that want analysts to build governed reports and everyone else to view them, at a low per-user price.

Why it made the shortlist

It has the deepest data modeling here for the lowest per-user price, and Pro is already included in Microsoft 365 E5.

Strengths

  • Pro at $14 a user a month, included in Microsoft 365 E5 and Office 365 E5
  • Free Power BI Desktop for modeling and building reports
  • Premium Per User raises model size from 1 GB to 100 GB for $24

Watch-outs

  • Every viewer needs Pro to see shared content, unless you buy F64 or larger Fabric capacity
  • Power BI Desktop runs on Windows only
  • Capacity and Embedded pricing is variable and quoted separately

Skip it if: Your team works on Macs and wants to build reports without a Windows machine.

What are business intelligence tools?

Business intelligence (BI) tools connect to databases, spreadsheets and business apps, model the data into consistent metrics, and present it as interactive dashboards and reports. Analysts build the models and dashboards; everyone else filters, drills down and gets scheduled reports or alerts, with permissions controlling who sees which rows.

Key features to look for

  • Data connectors. Built-in connections to databases, warehouses, files and SaaS apps, with scheduled refreshes.
  • Data modeling. Relationships, calculated measures and a shared semantic layer so every report uses the same numbers.
  • Dashboards and visualizations. Interactive charts, filters and drill-downs that people can explore without writing queries.
  • Sharing and governance. Workspaces, row-level security, SSO and audit logs that control who sees what.
  • AI and natural language queries. Ask questions in plain English, get suggested insights and automated summaries.

How to choose a business intelligence tool

  • Count authors and viewers separately. Tableau prices by role ($75 Creator, $42 Explorer, $15 Viewer), while Power BI charges $14 for every Pro user who shares or views shared content. Qlik Standard and Domo don't charge per user at all.
  • Check what the free version can share. Data Studio is free for creators and viewers; Metabase open source is free if you host it; Zoho Analytics Free covers 2 users. Power BI's free account is for personal use only.
  • Size your data. Power BI Pro models are capped at 1 GB of memory (100 GB on Premium Per User); Zoho Analytics caps rows by plan (0.5 to 5 million); Qlik Starter is fixed at 10 GB.
  • Find where governance starts. Row-level permissions and SSO are on Metabase Pro ($575 a month); Tableau's Data Management needs Enterprise; Data Studio's admin controls need Pro ($9 a user).
  • Plan for annual contracts. Tableau and Qlik bill annually only; Power BI Pro is shown paid yearly; Metabase offers monthly ($100) or annual ($1,080 a year) Starter billing.

Business intelligence FAQ

What is the best business intelligence tool?

Microsoft Power BI scores highest in our ranking at 92/100, with sharing on Pro at $14 a user a month. Tableau (83/100) rates highest on visualization, and Qlik Cloud Analytics (82/100) suits large audiences because users cost nothing extra from its Standard plan.

Is there a free business intelligence tool?

Yes. Data Studio (formerly Looker Studio) is free for creators and viewers, Metabase's open-source edition is free to self-host with unlimited users, and Zoho Analytics Free covers 2 users and 10,000 rows. Power BI Desktop is free, but sharing needs Pro.

How much does BI software cost for 10 people?

About $1,680 a year on Power BI Pro (10 users at $14), $1,405 on Metabase Starter billed annually, $3,240 on Tableau Standard with 2 Creators and 8 Viewers, and $3,600 on Qlik Starter. Data Studio's standard version is free.

Power BI or Tableau?

Power BI for most companies: Pro is $14 a user against $75 for a Tableau Creator and $15 for a Viewer, and Pro is included in Microsoft 365 E5. Tableau is the pick for analyst teams that build many custom visual dashboards. Our Power BI vs Tableau comparison prices both for 10 and 25 users.
