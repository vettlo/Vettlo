Clockify

Clockify is a time tracker from CAKE.com with timers, timesheets, a kiosk and reports. The free plan covers up to 5 users with unlimited tracking and projects; Basic ($3.99 per seat billed annually) adds billable rates; Standard ($5.49) adds approvals, invoicing, time off and locked timesheets; Pro ($7.99) adds budgets, GPS, scheduling, expenses and labor cost; Enterprise ($11.99) adds SSO, SCIM and an audit log.

Who it's best for

Small and mid-size teams that want timesheets, approvals and invoicing for the lowest per-seat price.

Strengths

  • Approvals, invoicing and locked timesheets from $5.49 a seat billed annually
  • Free plan for up to 5 users with unlimited projects
  • SSO, SCIM and audit log on Enterprise ($11.99)

Watch-outs

  • Budgets, GPS and activity monitoring need Pro ($7.99)
  • Every invited user counts as a paid seat
  • Free trial lasts 7 days, the shortest here

Skip it if: You want time captured automatically from your apps rather than started with a timer.

Who it's best for

Agencies, consultants and teams that want people to actually track their time, with minimal friction.

  • Rated 4.5/5 on G2 across 1,684 reviews

Watch-outs

  • Free plan stops at 3 users
  • Approvals and SSO need Premium ($16 annually, $24 monthly)
  • Time off management and capacity planning are add-ons ($2 and $5 a user)

Skip it if: You need approvals and SSO on a tight budget.

  • Smallest G2 review base here (183 reviews)

FAQ

What are the best time tracking tools for small businesses?

Clockify scores highest (89/100) once price and ease of use carry more weight, followed by Toggl Track, Everhour.

How is this different from your main ranking?

Same tools and the same 0 to 5 ratings, but price and ease of use count for more, because small teams rarely have an admin or a big budget. The weights are in the table on this page.

Which time tracking tools are cheapest for a small team?

See the starting prices in the comparison table; free plans and trials are listed next to them. Price the plan that covers your team size, not only the entry plan.

What should a small business look for?

A free plan or low entry price, quick setup without an admin, and the two or three features you need on day one. Avoid paying for enterprise controls you won't use yet.
