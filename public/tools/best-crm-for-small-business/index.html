How to choose a CRM for a small business

  1. Start with who will use it daily. If it's two founders, a free plan may be enough. If it's a sales team, pick the plan that includes the follow-up automation you need.
  2. Price the plan you'll need in six months, not the entry plan. Automation, forecasting and custom reports are usually one or two tiers up.
  3. Test with real deals. Import 20 live deals during the trial and run your normal week in the tool.
  4. Check the exit. Make sure you can export contacts, deals and activity history before you commit.

Small business CRM FAQ

What is the best CRM for a small business?

For most small sales teams, Pipedrive: it scores highest in our small-business ranking (87/100) because reps adopt it quickly and it lists sequences, automation and forecast reports on its $39 Growth plan. If you need a free start, HubSpot's free plan covers up to 2 users.

What is the best free CRM for a small business?

HubSpot's free plan covers up to 2 users, Zoho CRM's free edition covers up to 3 users, and Attio's free plan covers up to 3 seats. Pipedrive, Freshsales, Close and folk offer free trials instead of free plans.

How much should a small business spend on a CRM?

Entry plans on our list run from $7 to $35 per user per month on annual billing, so a 5-person team pays roughly $420 to $2,100 in year one. Plans with sales automation usually cost $35 to $100 per user per month.

Does a small business need a CRM?

Once more than one person is following up with leads or customers, a shared CRM stops deals from slipping through spreadsheets and inboxes. Many teams start on a free plan and move to paid when they need automation.

Is Salesforce good for small businesses?

Salesforce Starter Suite is priced for small teams, and it suits companies that expect to grow quickly. Most small teams without an admin will find Pipedrive, HubSpot or Zoho faster to set up.
