Short answer

Clockify is our top pick among the 7 time tracking tools we ranked, scoring 89/100. Paid plans start at $3.99/user/mo.

  • Clockify (89/100): best overall and best value for most teams.

Time tracking software records how long people spend on clients, projects and tasks, and turns those hours into timesheets, invoices and profitability reports. Every tool here has a timer and basic reports, so the differences are in the limits: how many users the free plan covers (1 to unlimited), which plan first adds billable rates, timesheet approvals and project budgets, and whether you want employee monitoring such as screenshots and activity levels.

We scored seven time trackers on six weighted criteria. For each one we list the free plan, the entry paid price on annual and monthly billing, and the plan where approvals, budgets and SSO start.

What is time tracking software?

Time tracking software records the hours people spend on clients, projects and tasks, with timers, manual timesheets or automatic activity capture. It turns those hours into approved timesheets, billable amounts, invoices and reports on budgets and profitability.

Key features to look for

  • Timers and timesheets. Start and stop timers on desktop, mobile or browser, or enter hours in a weekly grid.
  • Billable and cost rates. Rates per client, project, task or person, so hours turn into revenue and labor cost.
  • Timesheet approvals. Managers review and lock submitted hours before billing or payroll.
  • Budgets and invoicing. Project budgets with alerts, and invoices built from tracked time and expenses.
  • Reports and integrations. Hours by client or project, plus links to project management, accounting and payroll tools.

How to choose time tracking software

  • Count the users who will track time. Free plans range from 1 seat (Harvest, Hubstaff) and 3 users (Toggl) to 5 users (Clockify, Everhour) and unlimited users (TimeCamp). Timely has none.
  • Find where approvals start. Timesheet approvals are on Clockify Standard ($5.49), Everhour Team ($8.50), TimeCamp Ultimate ($9.99), Hubstaff Team ($10), Harvest Enterprise ($14) and Toggl Premium ($16), all billed annually.
  • Check billable rates and invoicing if you bill clients. Harvest includes invoicing on every plan; Clockify adds billable rates on Basic and invoicing on Standard. TimeCamp keeps billable rates for Ultimate.
  • Decide how much monitoring you want. Hubstaff and TimeCamp offer screenshots and app tracking; Timely tracks activity in the background for timesheets. Toggl, Harvest and Everhour are timer-based.
  • Price annual and monthly billing. Monthly billing costs about 18% to 50% more: Toggl Premium is $16 billed annually and $24 monthly, and Clockify Standard $5.49 against $6.99.

Time tracking FAQ

What is the best time tracking software?

Clockify scores highest in our ranking at 89/100, with approvals and invoicing from $5.49 a seat billed annually. Toggl Track (87/100) has the easiest timer, and Everhour (85/100) is best if you work in Asana or Jira.

What is the best free time tracker?

TimeCamp's free plan has unlimited users and projects. Clockify and Everhour are free for up to 5 users, Toggl for up to 3, and Harvest and Hubstaff for 1 seat. Timely has no free plan.

How much does time tracking software cost?

Paid plans start at $3.99 a user a month billed annually (Clockify Basic, TimeCamp Starter). Plans with timesheet approvals run from $5.49 (Clockify Standard) to $16 (Toggl Premium) a user billed annually, so 10 users cost $659 to $1,920 a year.

Which time trackers take screenshots?

Hubstaff includes screenshots on every plan, capped at 100, 500 and 1,500 per seat a month on Free, Starter and Grow. TimeCamp includes them on Ultimate ($9.99), and Clockify's activity monitoring is on Pro ($7.99).
