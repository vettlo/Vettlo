Short answer

Jira is our top pick among the 7 issue tracking tools we ranked, scoring 87/100. Paid plans start at $9.70/user/mo.

  • Jira (87/100): best overall for scaling engineering teams.
  • Linear (86/100): best for fast-moving product teams.

  • GitHub Issues (84/100): best for teams whose code is already on GitHub.

An issue tracker is where engineering teams log bugs, plan sprints and see what ships next. The tools here all handle the basics, so the differences are in the limits: how many users the free plan covers (5 to 10, or no cap with an issue limit), where sprints, roadmaps and reporting start, and whether you're already paying for one inside GitHub, GitLab or Azure DevOps.

We scored seven issue trackers on six weighted criteria. For each one we list the free plan, the entry paid price and the plan where planning, insights and SAML SSO start.

What is issue tracking software?

Issue tracking software is where software teams log bugs, feature requests and tasks as issues, then assign, prioritize and move them through a workflow until they ship. Most trackers add sprint or cycle planning, links to commits and pull requests, and reports on how much work gets done each sprint.

Key features to look for

  • Issues and custom workflows. Bugs, stories and tasks with statuses, priorities, assignees and custom fields.
  • Backlogs and sprints. A ranked backlog and time-boxed sprints or cycles to plan the next batch of work.
  • Boards and roadmaps. Kanban boards for daily work and timeline views for epics and releases.
  • Git integration. Commits, branches and pull requests linked to issues, often closing them automatically.
  • Reports and automation. Velocity and burndown charts, plus rules that update issues when something changes.

How to choose issue tracking software

  • Check what you already pay for. GitHub includes Issues and Projects on every plan, and Azure DevOps includes Boards in Basic with the first 5 users free. A separate tracker has to beat a tool you already have.
  • Size the free plan against your team. Jira, YouTrack and Shortcut cover 10 users, Azure DevOps 5 and GitLab 5 per group; Linear has no user cap but stops at 250 issues.
  • Price annual tiers, not just the monthly rate. Jira Standard is $9.70 per user monthly but $950 a year for 10 users on an annual plan; YouTrack drops from $5.40 to $4.50 billed annually.
  • Find where planning and reporting start. Jira's advanced planning is on Premium, Linear's Insights on Business and Shortcut's metrics dashboard on Business. YouTrack includes everything on every plan.
  • Ask when SAML SSO arrives. It's on Enterprise for Linear and GitHub ($21), an add-on on Shortcut Business, and part of Atlassian Guard, included with Jira Enterprise.

Issue tracking FAQ

What is the best issue tracking software?

Jira scores highest in our ranking at 87/100 for teams that need configurable workflows and cross-team planning. Linear (86/100) is the easiest to adopt, and YouTrack (85/100) is the best value at $4.50 per user billed annually.

What is the best free issue tracker?

Jira, YouTrack and Shortcut all have free plans for 10 users; YouTrack's includes every feature. GitHub includes Issues and Projects on its Free plan, Azure DevOps is free for the first 5 users, and Linear's free plan has unlimited members but a 250-issue limit.

How much does Jira cost?

From Oct 13, 2026, Jira Standard lists at $9.70 per user per month for 1 to 100 users on monthly billing, and Premium at $19.60. Annual plans are priced by user tier: $950 a year for 10 users or $2,450 for 25 users on Standard. The free plan covers 10 users.

Do I need a separate issue tracker if I use GitHub or GitLab?

Not always. GitHub Issues and Projects are free on every plan, with boards, roadmaps and iteration fields. GitLab's free plan includes issues for 5 users per group, but epics need Premium at $29 per user. Teams usually add Jira, Linear or YouTrack when they need more planning, reporting or non-engineering users.
