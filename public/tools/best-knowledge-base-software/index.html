Short answer

Notion is our top pick among the 7 knowledge base tools we ranked, scoring 87/100. Paid plans start at $10/seat/mo.

  • Notion (87/100): best overall for a flexible wiki with docs and databases.
  • Slab (84/100): best for a clean, low-cost team wiki.
  • Confluence (81/100): best for large teams and companies that use Jira.
  • Slite (80/100): best for AI answers from your docs on the entry plan.
  • Outline (78/100): best flat-priced or self-hosted wiki.

A knowledge base is where a team writes down how things work: processes, policies, onboarding docs and answers to the questions people ask every week. The tools below are internal wikis first. They differ most in three places: how they're priced (per user, per seat tier or a flat monthly fee), which plan first includes AI answers and SAML single sign-on, and how much they help you keep pages current with verification and stale-content reports.

We scored seven knowledge base tools on six weighted criteria. For each one we list what the free plan or trial includes, the entry paid price, and the plan where AI search, SSO and audit logs start, using only what each vendor's own pricing page showed on Nov 9, 2026.

What is knowledge base software?

Knowledge base software is a shared, searchable place where a team documents how it works: processes, policies, onboarding guides, product details and answers to common questions. Internal wikis organize pages into spaces or topics with permissions, and many now add AI that answers questions from the pages and tools that flag content for review before it goes out of date.

Key features to look for

  • Editor and structure. Rich pages organized into spaces, topics or collections, with templates.
  • Search and AI answers. Search across pages, and on many plans AI answers questions with links to the source docs.
  • Permissions and SSO. Private spaces, guest access, SAML single sign-on and SCIM user provisioning.
  • Verification and analytics. Page owners, review reminders and reports on stale or unread content.
  • Integrations. Slack answers, Jira or project links, and imports from other wikis and Google Docs.

How to choose knowledge base software

  • Price it at your team size. Per-user plans start at $6 (Nuclino), $6.67 (Slab, billed annually), $8 (Tettra, 10-user minimum) and $10 (Notion Plus, Slite Basic). Outline charges a flat $10 a month for up to 10 members and $79 for up to 100.
  • Check where AI answers start. Slite includes Ask on Basic ($10) and Outline on every cloud plan. Slab's AI Ask is on Business ($12.50), Notion's AI on Business ($20) and Nuclino's Sidekick on Business.
  • Find the plan with SAML SSO. Slab Business ($12.50) includes SAML SSO and SCIM, Notion Business ($20) SAML SSO, and Nuclino Business SAML SSO. Slite Pro has OpenID SSO, Tettra sells SSO as an add-on, and Confluence needs Enterprise or Atlassian Guard.
  • Plan for upkeep. A wiki goes stale fast. Slab has verification on its free plan, Slite and Tettra on their entry plans, and Notion on Business; Tettra also reports stale and unowned pages.
  • Count readers, not just writers. Nuclino counts guests and read-only members as users, Slite's reader-only seats need Enterprise, and Slab includes 3 guests per user on Startup and 5 on Business.

Knowledge Base Software FAQ

What is the best knowledge base software?

Notion scores highest in our ranking at 87/100, for teams that want a flexible wiki with docs and databases. Slab (84/100) is the best focused team wiki at a lower price, and Confluence (81/100) suits larger companies and teams that already use Jira.

Is there free knowledge base software?

Yes. Slab is free for up to 10 users with content verification, Confluence for up to 10 users with 2 GB of storage, Nuclino for up to 50 items and 3 canvases, and Notion has a free plan with a block limit for teams of 2 or more. Outline's self-hosted Community Edition is free if you run it yourself. Slite and Tettra offer 14-day and 30-day trials instead.

How much does knowledge base software cost?

Most internal wikis cost $6 to $20 per user a month. Nuclino starts from $6, Slab at $6.67 billed annually, Tettra at $8 with a 10-user minimum, and Notion Plus and Slite Basic at $10. Outline is priced per team instead: $10 a month for up to 10 members and $79 for up to 100.

Which knowledge base has the cheapest SAML SSO?

Slab, at $12.50 per user a month billed annually on Business, which also includes SCIM. Notion includes SAML SSO on Business at $20 a seat. Outline lists SSO on every cloud plan from $10 a month; Slite has OpenID SSO on Pro ($20), and Tettra sells SSO as an add-on.
