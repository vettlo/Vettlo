HubSpot vs Pipedrive: Which CRM Should Your Sales Team Pick in 2026?

By Vettlo Editorial TeamPrices verified Oct 14, 2026

We may earn a commission when you click some links on this page. It never changes our verdict. See how we make money.

Quick verdict

Pick Pipedrive if…

  • You run a pipeline-first sales team of roughly 3 to 25 reps
  • You need email sequences, automation and forecast reports without enterprise pricing
  • Nobody on the team has time to be a CRM admin

Pick HubSpot if…

  • Your marketing already runs on HubSpot, or will
  • You want a free or very cheap CRM for 1 or 2 users
  • You need deep custom reporting and can budget for Sales Hub Professional

In our CRM rankings, HubSpot Sales Hub scores 88/100 and Pipedrive 82/100. HubSpot wins on reporting and integrations; Pipedrive wins on rep adoption and value.

The short answer

The two CRMs split at the point where a team needs sales automation. HubSpot is cheaper to start: its free plan covers 2 users and Sales Hub Starter lists at $7 per seat per month on annual billing. But HubSpot lists email sequences and forecasting on Sales Hub Professional, at $90 per seat per month plus a $1,500 one-time onboarding fee. Pipedrive lists sequences, workflow automation and forecast reports on its Growth plan at $39 per seat per month. For a team that needs those features, Pipedrive costs well under half as much.

Where HubSpot is stronger

  • One database with marketing. If marketing uses HubSpot, leads, lifecycle stages and attribution flow into sales records without a sync to maintain.
  • A real free tier. Two users can run a pipeline at no cost, which is useful for founders before the first sales hire.
  • Reporting depth at Professional. Custom reports and forecasting at Professional go further than Pipedrive's equivalents.
  • Ecosystem. A large integration marketplace and partner network.

Where Pipedrive is stronger

  • Price of automation. Sequences, automation and forecast reports arrive on a $39 plan instead of a $90 plan with an onboarding fee.
  • Rep adoption. The visual pipeline and activity prompts keep reps focused on the next step on every deal, with little setup.
  • No admin required. Small teams can run it without a dedicated RevOps person.

How to decide in one afternoon

  1. List the three workflows your team can't live without, for example "follow-up sequence after a demo" or "weekly forecast call".
  2. Check which plan on each side includes all three. That's your real price, not the entry price.
  3. Multiply by your seat count for 12 months, and add HubSpot's onboarding fee if you land on Professional.
  4. Start both trials with a slice of your real pipeline and have two reps use each for a week.

HubSpot vs Pipedrive FAQ

Is HubSpot or Pipedrive cheaper?

At the entry level HubSpot is cheaper: Sales Hub Starter lists at $7 per seat per month billed annually, against $14 for Pipedrive Lite. Once you need email sequences, workflow automation and forecasting, Pipedrive is cheaper: those features are listed on Pipedrive Growth at $39 per seat per month, while HubSpot lists sequences and forecasting on Sales Hub Professional at $90 per seat per month plus a $1,500 one-time onboarding fee.

Does HubSpot have a free CRM?

Yes. HubSpot's free plan covers up to 2 users with no credit card required. Pipedrive has no free plan but offers a 14-day free trial on every paid plan.

Which is easier for sales reps to use?

Pipedrive is built around a visual deal pipeline and activity reminders, and small teams can usually work in it within a day. HubSpot is also approachable, but its wider feature set and shared marketing data take longer to configure well.

Should a team that uses HubSpot Marketing Hub pick HubSpot Sales Hub?

Usually yes. Sales and marketing then share one contact database, lifecycle stages and attribution, so RevOps doesn't have to maintain a sync between two systems.

Can I switch from Pipedrive to HubSpot later?

Yes, both tools support CSV import and export and have migration integrations. Plan field mapping, deduplication and activity history before the cutover, because history is the part teams most often lose.
